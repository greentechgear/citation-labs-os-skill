# Using the Citation Labs Guides With an Assistant

**A starting point for practitioners and people working through a shared decision**  
Version 0.1 | 10 September 2026 | Citation Labs

Use an assistant for the part of the investigation you need now. Start with your question and available evidence. The six tasks below can be used independently. \[G1-G4\]

## Choose the help you need

| Your question | Task | What comes back |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| What do these AI answers currently say and cite? | 01\. Establish a baseline | A scoped account of the answers, sources, measurement conditions, and missing evidence. |
| What information bears on the different responsibilities in this decision? | 02\. Investigate a shared decision | Comparable research and source findings, with possible human requirements clearly identified. |
| Which questions should we ask people with relevant experience? | 03\. Prepare interview questions | Questions connected to inspected passages, recorded searches, and the contribution a respondent could make. |
| What useful information can we produce from these accounts and sources? | 04\. Prepare information for use | A production brief or supported draft, with conditions, attribution, permissions, and review needs. |
| Did our information change help? | 05\. Plan or examine a test | A plan or readout separating human usefulness from changes in AI answers. |
| Could this result matter elsewhere in the organization? | 06\. Investigate a business consequence | A defined uncertainty, appropriate evidence requests, and the strongest supported account available. |

Describe the problem in ordinary language. The assistant can select the relevant task and guide and identify what the available evidence supports.

## Start with a concrete request

> Use the Citation Labs Task Instructions and the relevant guide to help me with \[question\]. The result needs to help \[person\] decide or do \[next action\]. Here are the records available: \[files or authorized locations\]. Start by reading them. Explain briefly what you will examine and return, then complete the work those records support. Identify any further evidence or approval needed for the remaining work.

For example: "Use Task 03 to prepare interview questions from these recorded searches and source lists. Focus on what someone with finance responsibility could explain about funding a program when referral traffic is small. Keep the original passages with the questions."

**Supply the relevant guide and Task Instructions.** For connected-tool work, also make the reference files accessible. The Source Index identifies the intended versions.

## Provide the material you already have

A useful starting description says what is happening, who has a part in it, what remains uncertain, and what the next answer would help someone do. Attach relevant records: exact prompts, AI answers and citations, source lists, documents, interview notes, or observations from people using information.

The guides serve different questions. **AI Visibility** examines the answer environment. **Helping People Work Through a Shared Decision** provides a focused research exercise across responsibilities. **Information Design for Humans at Work** examines what information must enable and whether a change helps. **Tracing Business Impact Beyond the Click** examines a possible organizational consequence. \[G1-G4\]

A shared decision can concern a household, education, work, or another setting. Asteron, the synthetic company in the research note, is the continuing worked example; wider uses remain applications to evaluate. \[G2, Orientation\]

### Instructions, connections, and exports do different jobs

**Instructions explain how to work.** Giving the assistant a guide or tool briefing supplies a method and reference material.

**A connection provides access.** A configured tool may allow the assistant to retrieve existing research or submit approved new work. The administrator supplies access instructions, charges, and data-handling terms.

**Existing files can be enough.** An export containing the necessary prompts, answers, queries, or sources may support the task without a live connection. The assistant should identify the coverage it actually has. \[T1, sections 2-3 and 5b; T2, sections 3 and 5b\]

### Where Thinking Inspector and XOFU fit

Thinking Inspector, or TI, exposes recorded searches, retrieved source lists, citation flags, and answers for research runs. It helps examine how the recorded system researched a request. XOFU supports persistent prompt projects, repeated measurement across selected systems, and views of answers, citations, and other reported measures. Its documented interface leaves the wider retrieved source set unobserved. \[T1, sections 2-3; T2, sections 1 and 3\]

The assistant should use the capabilities needed for the question. An interview-record review, for example, may use neither instrument. Tool details here are dated accounts of inspected interfaces; the assistant checks the connection it actually has before executing calls.

## Expect the assistant to make the scope clear

Its opening should state the task, sources, planned result, and any consequential uncertainty. It should read accessible records before asking you to reconstruct them. A clarification is appropriate when the answer would change scope, permission, spending, or interpretation.

It should complete useful work supported by the evidence and identify the rest precisely. For example: "The source comparison is complete for the supplied runs. These three pages need inspection before their contents can support interview questions."

## Know where your participation matters

You establish your own circumstances, priorities, and choices. Other participants establish theirs. The assistant can organize accounts, identify differences, and prepare questions; people retain the judgments and permissions that belong to them. \[G2, Steps 1-2; G3, Parts I-II\]

Approve new paid research, recurring schedules, outreach, publication, and consequential changes to shared records separately from read-only analysis. An approval should identify the action and scope. In the documented XOFU interface, some actions create immediate or recurring charges and some changes need a different interface to reverse. \[T2, section 2\]

### A useful result shows its basis

The result should identify the question investigated, records examined, findings, supporting sources, remaining uncertainty, and what is ready to use. Detailed logs can be attached to the guide's existing working record.

Expect the assistant to distinguish:

- **A recorded observation:** what the tool, source, participant, or test actually reports.  
- **An interpretation or hypothesis:** what that evidence may mean, and what could strengthen or change the conclusion.  
- **A proposed action:** what to ask, check, produce, or test next, and why.

The guides' full evidence-state vocabulary remains available when a claim's strength matters. A tool label establishes that label was returned. Its meaning and any broader conclusion need their own support. \[G1, Part VIII; G2, Where this method fits\]

## Recognize a complete handoff

A completed task returns the requested working result with sources and limitations. A research plan is a valid deliverable when planning was the assignment. Analysis with missing records identifies its completed scope and the unresolved portion. A proposed interview or test remains proposed until someone carries it out.

Ask for the files you can use now. The assistant should report local-file completion and successful upload separately and provide a usable handoff once the work is ready. Further research begins when a remaining question justifies it.
