## AN APPLICATION OF INFORMATION DESIGN

### A focused investigation within the guide family

Information Design provides the purpose: understand what information must enable for people. This note teaches one applied research exercise within that practice. [<u>AI Visibility</u>](https://citationlabs.com/operating-system/field-guides/ai-visibility/) provides methods for observing the AI answer environment; Business Impact investigates organizational consequence when that question arises. The three guides retain their existing responsibilities. [R01–R03]

| **Question in front of you** | **Where to continue, when relevant** |
| --- | --- |
| What do people need information to enable, and does a change help their work? | [<u>Information Design for Humans at Work</u>](https://citationlabs.com/operating-system/field-guides/information-design/). This note specializes its tool-led, role-by-role research. |
| What is the AI answer environment doing, and did an intervention change it? | [<u>AI Visibility</u>](https://citationlabs.com/operating-system/field-guides/ai-visibility/). |

### Distinguish tool observations, source findings, and human requirements

The AI research record establishes what the recorded system searched for, retrieved, cited, or said under the specified conditions.

Source inspection establishes what a document contains and what its evidence supports. The source’s account and independent verification of that account are distinct findings.

A proposed requirement describes what a person may need information to enable. When it comes only from prompt assumptions or AI behavior, it remains a hypothesis. Records, expertise, direct inquiry, or other evidence about the people doing the work can strengthen, qualify, or overturn it. [R01, R02]

### Use the family’s evidence states

| **State** | **Meaning** |
| --- | --- |
| OBSERVED / DOCUMENTED | Directly observed, measured, recorded, documented, or established through inspection. |
| MODELED / ESTIMATED | Produced through modeling, weighting, extrapolation, forecasting, or estimation. |
| INFERRED | A reasonable conclusion from evidence that was not directly observed. |
| HYPOTHESIS | A proposed explanation, requirement, mechanism, or expected effect that still needs evidence. |
| UNKNOWN | The available evidence does not support a stronger classification. |

Provenance means where a statement comes from: for example, a prompt, a run record, an inspected passage, or a conversation. Record it beside the statement. Use an evidence state when the strength of a claim matters to the next decision. [R01-R03]

## 1. FRAME THE SHARED DECISION

### Describe one decision and one common situation

Start with one consequential choice or commitment. Name who can make or authorize it, who contributes judgment or practical support, and who will live with its effects. These parts may overlap. Record the circumstances, the conditions that matter, and the realistic alternatives. Adapt the scope to the decision in front of you, whether it concerns work, education, a household, or another setting.

Throughout this note, the worked example is **Asteron Industrial Systems**, a synthetic industrial company deciding what to do next with an AI-visibility program. The program has produced encouraging visibility results while direct referral traffic remains small and downstream business effects remain uncertain. The CMO must recommend whether to continue, expand, change, or reallocate the investment - and prepare that recommendation for serious scrutiny from colleagues with different responsibilities. [[R04-R06]](#ifimbnt8y3w0)

The Asteron decision used throughout this note is:

**What should Asteron do with the next period of AI-visibility investment, and what evidence would help the people involved evaluate that commitment well?**

Separate the shared decision from the research decision. The people in the situation make the shared choice. The person using this method decides what to research, inspect, verify, or ask next. In Asteron, the company decides what to fund; the investigator decides what evidence can responsibly be carried into that discussion.

### Write down the common situation once

| **Record once** | **What to include** |
| --- | --- |
| Choice or proposed commitment | What is being considered, by whom, and over what period? Include the option of keeping current arrangements where relevant. |
| Governing context, if relevant | Name any regulatory, contractual, standards, or policy regime that materially shapes the decision, or record it as deliberately open. |
| Available alternatives | Proceed, revise the proposal, choose another option, retain current arrangements, defer, or stop, as appropriate to this decision. |
| Material conditions | Costs, time, practical commitments, eligibility, dependencies, relevant results, and constraints. Identify personal priorities separately from facts that an external source can establish. |
| Facts and basis | Which details come from records, people involved, qualified experts, inspected sources, a teaching case, or investigator assumptions? |
| Open questions | Which facts, causal relationships, and requirements remain uncertain? |
| Scope of this pass | Which roles, tools, sources, dates, and comparisons will the investigation cover? |

Save one version of the common factual block and use it across the role prompts being compared. When a fact applies to everyone, keep it in that shared block. When a difference is specific to a represented responsibility, identify it explicitly. This lets the reader see which prompt features changed. The Asteron research used a frozen situation and explicit prompt-construction rules for this purpose. [R04, R05]

### Keep all realistic outcomes available

Ask what would justify each realistic option, including proceeding, revising, choosing an alternative, gathering more evidence, deferring, or stopping. Record the criteria people actually use when those are known. Identify proposed criteria as assumptions to check. [R01]

When external tools are used, use approved and appropriately scoped material. Keep private identities and sensitive facts out of external systems unless disclosure is authorized. Asteron is synthetic and exists to make the method inspectable.

WHAT TO DO Write the shared decision in one sentence. Save a dated version of the common facts and their sources. Name material rules or policies, or record them as unresolved. If the research assumes a governing context, record that assumption. State what this first research pass must help you decide to inspect, ask, or change.

## 2. NAME THE RESPONSIBILITIES

### Describe what each person is responsible for judging or contributing

In this note, a responsibility is the judgment, choice, task, or contribution being represented in a research prompt. A role is its label in the working record. Use the person's actual part in the situation where it is known; record a proposed part as a hypothesis. A job title is optional, and one person may have several responsibilities. [R01]

Record the distinction between decision authority, advice, practical contribution, and being affected by the result. People can share consequences while retaining different rights and choices. Ask them about the constraints, preferences, and commitments that research cannot establish for them. A shared decision may end in agreement, a revised option, a stated disagreement, or a decision to wait.

### Asteron example: the CMO prepares colleagues to review a recommendation

In the Asteron teaching case, the CMO must recommend what happens next with the AI-visibility program. She needs to understand the result and prepare information that colleagues can examine from their own responsibilities. This is one business application of the broader method.

In the Asteron example, the CMO needs to give each colleague enough of the record to ask the questions their responsibility requires. A colleague may support the recommendation, add a condition, correct it, or change it. The CEO then carries the resulting recommendation, including material disagreements and unresolved questions, into Board review.

| **Participant** | **What they contribute to the shared decision** | **What the CMO needs to help colleagues examine** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CMO | Recommend how the next marketing allocation should change. | What the program established, what it may justify, what is proposed, and what remains uncertain. |
| CFO | Judge what further spending would buy and what evidence would justify continuation, expansion, or termination. | Commitment, assumptions, alternatives, thresholds, and the evidence expected from another period of investment. |
| Legal | Judge what claims, substantiation, disclosures, and controls are warranted. | Which statements are being carried forward, their support, their conditions, and where specialist review is required. |
| Content / SEO / AEO | Judge what the recommendation requires from current work and which information changes are feasible. | The work to scope, dependencies, current commitments, and the evidence behind proposed changes. |
| CEO / Board | Judge whether the resulting recommendation deserves organizational commitment and oversight. | The proposed action, the reasons colleagues support or challenge it, its conditions, alternatives, and unresolved questions. |

These are modeled responsibilities in the Asteron study, rather than interview findings about particular people. The research examines what the selected system does when those responsibilities are supplied. [[R04-R06]](#ifimbnt8y3w0)

### Start with what you actually know about each responsibility

An existing record, conversation, practitioner account, or other evidence may ground a responsibility. You may also begin with a plausible responsibility to investigate. Preserve that difference. When the initial account is hypothetical, the research shows what follows from the modeled responsibility; it does not establish that the real person holds it.

The full Asteron experiment used eight organizational roles plus a Neutral condition. A live investigation can begin with the few responsibilities most likely to change the decision, recommendation, or questions that need to be resolved. [R05, R06]

WHAT TO DO For each represented role, state what the person needs to judge, choose, or contribute. Record the basis for that description, their authority or practical influence, and the questions about their part in the decision that remain open.

## 3. ASK COMPARABLE QUESTIONS

### Keep the situation the same while you change the responsibility being represented

Give each represented responsibility its own fresh AI research request. Preserve the common factual record and use a consistent evidence request. Change the responsibility clause deliberately and save its exact wording. This makes the resulting differences easier to inspect. [R04]

A small Asteron starting design can use the CMO, CFO, Legal, and Content conditions plus a role-agnostic Neutral baseline, all at the same knowledge level and decision moment. The baseline asks about the shared commitment while omitting explicit role identity. The full study expands this logic to 81 conditions. [[R05, R06]](#pjeuoaw8c5z5)

The baseline shows what the system does with the common situation alone. A role comparison adds both the title and the responsibility as written. Its result concerns that combined prompt condition. A question about a bare-title effect requires a separate test. [[R04, R06]](#ifimbnt8y3w0)

### Preserve what you put into the prompt

| **Prompt component** | **Keep or vary deliberately** |
| --- | --- |
| Shared decision and facts | Keep the same in a role comparison. |
| Responsibility and authority | Vary by role; save the exact clause and its evidence basis. |
| Requested decision moment | Keep the same when comparing roles. Interpretation, response, and action questions can be tested separately. |
| Evidence request | Keep consistent so the instruction to research or cite is comparable. |
| Model, search surface, settings, and time | Keep the same in a role comparison. Model and search backend can be varied as a separate comparison when the instrument supports it; hold everything else fixed. A difference between two systems bundles their models, indexes, and retrieval policies together, so describe it as a difference between systems, not between models. |

### Add more context only when it answers a specific research question

A second comparison can ask what changes when more of the situation is specified. Preserve the original prompt and add the extra facts. Keep a record of the addition, including uncertainty and alternative explanations. Compare levels within the same role and decision moment. [[R04]](#ifimbnt8y3w0)

Use additional facts to investigate a defined question. Costs, timing, relevant conditions, constraints, and alternative explanations may change the research. Save the added material exactly, including any named sources or methods. In Asteron, the protocol added context while leaving the system to select methods and publishers. [R04, R05]

A separate comparison can examine different research systems. Use the same prompt family and record the model, search service, settings, and dates for each. Asteron used one documented system; comparisons across systems remain further work. [R06]

### Decide repetitions based on what you want to claim

One run can provide an initial source list to inspect. Repeating the same condition helps reveal ordinary variation. Choose the repetition plan to match the claim, stakes, and available resources. Preserve failed runs and runs with no visible search. One run per exact condition remains exploratory. [R02, R06]

**WHAT TO DO** Save the prompt family and common record before collection. Label the comparison you are making: role, supplied knowledge, decision moment, or system.

## A RESEARCH PROMPT YOU CAN ADAPT

### Start with the shared decision, then describe one responsibility

WORKING TEMPLATE. Use this structure for a new investigation. The historical Asteron workbook remains the exact instrument for reproducing the reported experiment. [[R04, R05]](#ifimbnt8y3w0)

We are investigating the information available around this shared decision: [decision].

Here is the common situation record: [paste the approved facts, their conditions, and the important unknowns].

Approach this from the perspective of [person or role]. This person is responsible for [specific judgment or task] within the shared decision. Their authority, practical influence, and relevant constraints are [details, with assumptions identified].

Investigate the questions that arise for that responsibility. Use current external evidence where relevant and cite the sources you rely on. Explain which statements the sources support, which conditions affect applicability, and what remains unresolved.

Distinguish information supplied in this prompt, information added by external sources, and your interpretation of the situation. State what the person may need to establish and the basis for each proposed requirement. Describe what further evidence would change the assessment.

For a role-agnostic baseline, keep the common facts and evidence request the same while leaving out a particular responsibility. Ask what needs to be established for the shared decision. Save this prompt with the role-specific versions.

### Asteron responsibility clauses

| **Role** | **Example responsibility clause** |
| --- | --- |
| CMO | Assess what the current evidence justifies recommending about the next marketing allocation, including what remains uncertain. |
| CFO | Assess what would justify further spending and what evidence would support continuation, expansion, revision, or termination. |
| Legal | Assess which claims, substantiation, disclosure, and governance questions must be resolved before the recommendation travels onward. |
| Content | Assess which evidence gaps warrant content work, which current commitments would be affected, and what dependencies matter. |

These summarize the responsibilities represented in the Asteron design; use the frozen workbook for the executed wording. The role clause supplies a concern to the model. The research then shows what the system adds, qualifies, or challenges within that framing. [[R04-R06]](#ifimbnt8y3w0)

### Separate the question from the instrument

The prompt asks for research and a sourced answer. The instrument determines which search queries, surfaced URLs, explicit opens, and citation records can actually be captured. Save exposed tool events through the instrument; treat a model-written account of its searches as a statement to verify against the available trace.

**WHAT TO DO** Run each condition independently. Preserve the exact prompt, returned answer, exposed events, and run identifier before asking an assistant to summarize or compare them.

## HOW THE ASSISTANT AND RESEARCH TOOL WORK TOGETHER

### Four different jobs in one investigation

You frame the decision and approve the comparison. Your assistant helps prepare requests and work with the returned records. The research instrument executes prompts and exposes activity. The investigator then checks the records, reads sources, and states what the evidence supports.

MCP, or Model Context Protocol, is the connection through which an assistant application accesses an external tool. The tool supplies its particular functions and records. [[R22]](#1z65fmdtyk2f)

| **Participant or component** | **Its job here** |
| --- | --- |
| Practitioner | Define the shared decision, common facts, responsibilities, and evidence request. |
| Assistant, such as Claude | Organize the approved prompts, request runs or stored results, and assist with comparison and reading. |
| Thinking Inspector and the selected research system | In the documented implementation, TI submits prompts to an OpenAI model with API web search and exposes returned records. This is the API environment being studied. [[R21]](#2mdmltw41ejy) |
| Investigator | Verify the record, inspect underlying sources, and keep the tool observation, source finding, and human requirement distinct. |

### Choose between a new run and a new analysis

A new research run tests a new condition or repeats an existing one. A new analysis can instead revisit stored runs: compare another pair, inspect an uncited source, or examine a qualification in an answer. Preserve the original prompt, settings, and run identifier in either case. [[R21]](#2mdmltw41ejy)

### Separate capture, recovery, and preservation

Capture is the record the instrument exposes. Recovery is how the assistant or client obtains that record. Preservation is how the returned data are saved for later inspection. Check each capability. Keep this use of recovery distinct from retrieved sources, which refers to URLs encountered during the model's research.

For the dated Thinking Inspector implementation used in this research, see Appendix K. It covers the recorded tool functions, response formats, existing-run recovery, settings, and the remaining access and archival questions.

**WHAT TO DO** Identify who designs the investigation, which system performs the research, which records are available, and how the records will be preserved.

## 4. CAPTURE WHAT THE TOOL EXPOSES

### Establish what is observable before comparing runs

Begin with a capability check. An instrument runs a prompt against a research system and exposes some record of what happened. Describe those capabilities separately from the route used to recover and save the record.

**Capture.** Record access to the exact prompt and settings, search queries, original source URLs, explicit open/find actions where exposed, cited URLs, full answer, stable run identifier, and any query-to-URL linkage.

**Complete recovery.** Establish whether the relevant response is full or condensed, whether results are paginated, and how returned counts are reconciled with the expected total.

**Preservation.** Demonstrate a direct route for saving complete returned data, through a native export or an authorized client-side capture. Preserve original response data separately from analytical tables. When a route remains unresolved, keep the authoritative run identifiers and state the archival gap.

The dated TI briefing documents the available responses and reports no file-writing command among the seven tools visible to that operator. Access to a full answer and a tested way to save it are separate capabilities. Appendix K explains both, with their verification status. [[R21]](#2mdmltw41ejy)

Use the records actually available for the comparison they can support. [<u>AI Visibility</u>](https://citationlabs.com/operating-system/field-guides/ai-visibility/) provides the broader tool-evaluation method. [[R02]](#g0g5kjuvydaq)

| **Record** | **What it establishes in this investigation** |
| --- | --- |
| Exact prompt and run settings | The constructed condition that produced the response. |
| Visible search queries | What the system visibly asked its search tool. |
| Retrieved source set | The surfaced or consulted URLs under the instrument’s declared definition. |
| Explicit page-open events | An additional recorded action, where the tool exposes it. |
| Cited URLs | Sources displayed as support in the final answer. |
| Final answer | The statements, conditions, calculations, and recommendation the system expressed. |
| Analyst source inspection | What the investigator subsequently read and could verify in the sources. |

In this note, retrieved means the surfaced or consulted URLs exposed for a run. The original Asteron dataset separately labels SURFACED as true and RETRIEVED as NOT\_OBSERVABLE. Preserve those raw values. Explicitly opened remains a separate action flag. Appendix E records the full terminology and historical examples. [R07]

A citation can appear without a separately recorded page open. An answer-only tool may reveal citations while leaving the wider retrieval set unknown. Capture the layers actually exposed, then describe the analysis at that scope. [[R02, R07]](#g0g5kjuvydaq)

A matched Asteron slice shows what a compact capture inventory can look like. These four L1 DECIDE runs share the same decision moment and knowledge level; only the represented responsibility changes. [[R06, R07]](#bpfxj86y64sj)

The difference in visible search activity is itself part of the record. Explicit opens are optional recorded actions, rather than a stage every citation must pass through. The cited-set differences below are therefore interpreted from the surfaced, opened, cited, and answer records separately. [[R06, R07]](#bpfxj86y64sj)

### Keep prompts, runs, and source records linked

Use a stable run identifier connecting the prompt, role, common-record version, settings, answer, and URLs. Preserve original URL strings; record any grouping of subdomains, aliases, or page variants separately. For a larger collection, one row per run and original URL makes recurrence and source states easier to compare. [[R07]](#3wzhf4b96orf)

Keep the model's page-opening events separate from the investigator's later reading. Connect a specific query to a URL only when the instrument exposes that link. In Asteron, queries and source sets are available by run, while a reliable query-to-URL link for each source remains unavailable. [R06, R07]

### Handle a run with no visible external research

Retain the result as part of the record. A further prompt variant can test another question about retrieval, and a separately logged source check can investigate the evidence directly. Each is a new research action. The original condition continues to describe the original run.

**WHAT TO DO** Save a simple capture inventory: available layers, missing layers, instrument definition, collection date, and any anomalies. Then begin the role comparison.

## 5. COMPARE THE SOURCES AND READ THEM

### Start by listing the questions the system actually investigated

**Query fan-out (QFO)** is the set of smaller search queries an AI system generates while answering the larger prompt you supplied. When the instrument records them, these queries are concrete **mechanical information-demand signals**: requests for information made by the system under the recorded situation and responsibility. Read the wording, including any named source, alongside the original prompt. It shows what was searched for; whether the sources answer it and whether people need that information are separate questions. [[R02, R06, R28]](#g0g5kjuvydaq)

For each represented responsibility, summarize the main questions visible in the trace and keep the raw strings available. Separate concerns already assigned in the prompt from new conditions, exceptions, methods, or source questions introduced during research.

For example, a role clause that explicitly asks about claim substantiation already supplies that concern. The useful additional learning is which evidence the system seeks, which distinctions the sources support, and what it leaves unresolved. Asteron’s role conditions deliberately combined accountability and situation; the resulting differences belong to that construction. [[R04, R06]](#ifimbnt8y3w0)

### Compare sources at more than one level

A domain identifies a host or source organization; a page identifies a selected resource; a passage shows what a source actually says. Compare at the level needed for your question. Shared domains may contain different pages or qualifications. Read the relevant material before interpreting similarity or difference as useful to a person. [R13, R18]

| **View** | **Useful question** |
| --- | --- |
| Shared sources | Which pages recur across roles? Do they support the same statement for each role? |
| Role-specific selections | Which pages appear for one represented responsibility? What specific claim or condition can each source establish? |
| Retrieved and cited | Which sources appeared in the wider record, and which were displayed as support? |
| Answer and cited passage | Does the cited material support the statement made, at the scope represented? |

A count is optional when direct inspection is enough. For larger sets, the following section describes the measures and their limits. State whether the items are URLs, domains, or publisher groups, and how URLs were normalized. [[R06]](#bpfxj86y64sj)

## PRIORITIZE FREQUENTLY RETRIEVED, UNCITED SOURCES

**When a relevant page or domain appears repeatedly in retrieval but rarely or never appears in the same runs' citations, give it priority for inspection. Pay particular attention when a QFO explicitly names the source.** Use this as a lead for developing questions that a subject-matter expert or someone with relevant experience could help answer.

For example, a generated search might contain reddit or site:reddit.com. The first names Reddit in the search text; the second asks for results from that domain. Check the full query: a search about Reddit policy and a search for firsthand experience on Reddit ask for different information. These examples explain what to look for; they are not quotations from a specific Asteron run.

| **What to look for** | **What to do with it** |
| --- | --- |
| Repeated retrieval | Compare page or domain appearances with citations in the same selected runs. Distinguish one page returning repeatedly from many different pages on one domain. |
| An explicit source name in a QFO | Save the exact query and its run ID. Check what information it requests and whether pages from the named source appear in that run. |
| Few or zero citations | Inspect the relevant retrieved pages even though the final citations omit them. Look for questions, claims, reported experiences, and disagreements. |

State the scope and counting unit: runs containing the source, distinct pages, or page appearances across runs. In Asteron, the reported 2,111 Reddit retrieval records and zero cited records count run-by-URL appearances across the batch. They identify a source pool to inspect, rather than 2,111 distinct threads or proof that each query requested Reddit. [[R06, R07]](#bpfxj86y64sj)

### Read the pages to develop useful questions

Read the original question, substantive responses, and relevant disagreements. Look for a number with an unclear basis, a workaround, a decision criterion, a reported result, or a condition that changes the answer. A page may already contain an informative account. Your next question can ask someone to explain its circumstances, supply supporting records, or contribute contrasting experience. [[R29]](#p9gwd6ae34mi)

Suppose an inspected thread describes a team continuing a visibility program because of sales-team feedback despite small referral numbers. Commenters disagree about whether that feedback justified the spending. This hypothetical thread could prompt the question below; it is an illustration, not a finding from an inspected Asteron page.

*"Have you used sales-team feedback when deciding whether to continue a marketing program? Describe a specific decision. What did the feedback establish, how did you check it, and what other evidence affected your choice?"*

Keep the QFO, run identifiers, URL, relevant passage, and resulting interview question together. Ask the respondent for their own evidence and experience, including accounts that challenge the original claim. Preserve the original source's contribution and distinguish what the interview independently adds.

The recorded pattern establishes search, retrieval, and citation status. The source's influence on the original answer and the reason it was uncited remain open. Link a query directly to a page only where the instrument records that relationship; otherwise describe their connection as your assessment within the run. [[R06, R07]](#bpfxj86y64sj)

**WHAT TO DO:** Flag recurring retrieved-but-uncited sources, especially those explicitly named in QFOs. Read the relevant pages and prepare questions that could add explanation, supporting records, or contrasting experience. Step 8 shows how to choose respondents, use their accounts, and assess any resulting publication.

### Read each source for what it actually establishes

Record the specific claim, its supporting passage or data, the source’s basis, and the applicable date, version, setting, and limits. Note sponsorship and editorial control where they affect the source relationship. A manufacturer can establish its stated capability or terms; an independent test can establish an observed result under its test conditions; an official document can establish a requirement within its remit.

**WHAT TO DO** For each material source, write: “This page supports \_\_\_\_ under \_\_\_\_ conditions. It leaves \_\_\_\_ unresolved for the represented responsibility.”

## HOW WE COMPARE CITATION SETS

### Sources used together and sources shared across answers

This method compares the sources that role-conditioned answers cite. Two established bibliometric ideas describe that comparison, and they answer different questions.

**Sources used together.** Co-citation counts how often two sources are cited together; Small (1973) introduced the measure. Here one answer supplies one co-occurrence. Across answers, those occurrences can show recurring bundles of displayed evidence. Inspect the claims those sources support before interpreting the bundle. [[R23]](#c95czyn3bejz)

Sources shared across answers. Bibliographic coupling relates documents through shared references (Kessler, 1963). Here, the documents are AI-generated answers. Asteron's Jaccard comparisons measure how much their cited URL sets overlap. This describes selected evidence under the recorded conditions. [R06, R23]

For example, the Asteron CMO and Content L1 DECIDE answers share two cited URLs. That is a comparison across answers. A co-citation question instead asks which sources appear together within one answer, and whether that pairing recurs across answers. Answer those questions separately. [R06, R07]

### How this application differs from the original bibliometric use

The application here uses deliberately constructed prompts: the recorded system, shared situation, and evidence request are held fixed while responsibility wording varies. The result describes those generated answers. Use the source map to guide source inspection and provisional role requirements; direct claims about people require evidence about their work.

### Say exactly what you are counting

State whether you are counting exact URL strings, pages, hostnames, or publisher groups. A query parameter or alternate address can make the same page appear as two exact URLs. Grouping at domain level answers a broader question about which organizations appear. Keep the original addresses and document every grouping rule. [R07, R13, R18]

Asteron's worked comparison uses exact cited URL strings. Apply that rule consistently when reproducing its reported values. For a new analysis, decide in advance whether to combine page variants, tracking parameters, trailing slashes, or mirror hosts; then report the rule with the result. [R06, R07]

### Explain the denominator and the limits of an overlap measure

Jaccard divides the number of shared items by all distinct items in the two sets. In the Asteron CMO-Content comparison, the answers cite 12 and 9 URLs and share 2. Their union contains 19 URLs, so Jaccard is 2/19, or 0.105. The overlap coefficient instead divides by the smaller set: 2/9, or approximately 0.222. It answers how much of the smaller set is shared. Report the set sizes with either measure. [R06, R07]

A zero-overlap pair, a both-empty pair, and a pair where one condition performed no external search are three different observations. Record each as what it is.

### State only what the comparison itself establishes

A cited-set difference establishes that the recorded system displayed different supporting sources under the compared prompts. Claims about which sources people would use, source quality, or causal influence require additional evidence. Examine answers separately: in Asteron, different cited sets coexisted with the analyst-reported broad funding recommendation. [R06-R08]

**WHAT TO DO For each comparison, record the counting unit, normalization rule, measure, set sizes, and whether you are describing sources used together within an answer or sources shared across answers. Then record the specific question the difference makes worth checking.**

## DISTINGUISH THE PROMPT, THE TOOL RECORD, AND THE FINDING

### Show how each interpretation is supported

A material finding should show how you moved from the prompt to the interpretation. Keep four layers separate: what the prompt supplied, what the tool recorded, what source inspection supports, and what you infer about the person or next action. [R01, R02]

| **Layer** | **Asteron example** |
| --- | --- |
| What we supplied | The CMO must decide how the next marketing allocation should change. |
| What the tool did | In CMO\_DECIDE\_L1, it issued 12 search queries, surfaced 106 URLs, explicitly opened 4, and cited 12. Searches included AI-summary click behavior, B2B buyer research, AI referral traffic, and engineering-buyer information sources. Run ID: 34eba758-3b72-4392-b3d2-ef4e02801874. [[R06, R07]](#bpfxj86y64sj) |
| What source inspection can support | A cited source can establish its own measured result, policy, or published finding under stated conditions. The practitioner must read the source before carrying that claim into the role finding. |
| What we infer for the role | The CMO may need to explain the visibility result, measurement limits, proposed expenditure, and questions requiring colleagues' judgment together. This is a HYPOTHESIS about the information that may help her, rather than a finding about a real CMO. |

### Describe coverage accurately

Use statements such as “The sources inspected in this pass provide X and leave Y unresolved.” Record what was inspected, how it was found, and what remained inaccessible. A broader claim about information being absent requires a broader search and a clear account of its scope.

A source can appear in the retrieved record without appearing among the answer's citations. Record that distinction directly. Relevance, credibility, repetition, snippets, and answer construction are possible explanations to investigate; the presence and citation flags leave the selection reason unresolved. [R06, R07]

### Look for corrections and contrary evidence

Read sources that qualify a convenient claim as carefully as those supporting it. Check whether seemingly independent pages rely on the same original study. Preserve substantive differences between settings or versions. A disagreement may identify a condition that changes the evidence requirement rather than a contest one source must win. [[R01]](#vwx14kzg8vk)

### Keep the work proportionate

Go deeper when an answer could change the next research step, conversation, verification, or publishing decision. A modest correction may already have enough support. Budget time for source reading as well as tool execution.

**WHAT TO DO Preserve the sequence from supplied premise to tool behavior to source support to a provisional requirement or next action. State the unresolved step plainly.**

## 6. WRITE EACH ROLE’S FINDINGS

### Write a findings page that another person can check

The first deliverable is one evidence account for each represented responsibility. It should let another person see what the investigation found, what sources support it, and how tentative any proposed human requirement remains.

Use one role findings page within the Decision Evidence Record. Reference the common situation rather than repeating it. Keep the role clause and its evidence basis alongside the result.

| **Include** | **What it contributes** |
| --- | --- |
| Responsibility supplied | Identifies the judgment or contribution described in the prompt, including assumptions about the person's authority or practical influence. |
| Research additions or challenges | Shows new conditions, questions, or exceptions. Preserve any relevant QFO, run ID, and inspected source passage behind a proposed interview question. |
| Recorded source behavior | Preserves queries, retrieved and cited sets where available, and the expressed answer. |
| Inspected source support | Names the precise statement supported, its basis, conditions, and limits. |
| Possible information requirement | States what the person may need to establish and the evidence state of that conclusion. |
| Information for another person | Names who receives the result next and what must stay attached. |
| Next check | Identifies the uncertainty to resolve, who could answer it, and what evidence would make their answer useful. |
| What this raises for the person in this role | The questions the pass could not settle about the person, the setting, or a source’s applicability. Usable in an interview, a survey, a review meeting, or as the record of what remains open when none of those is available. |

### State provisional requirements as provisional

A useful form is: “For [person or role] considering [judgment within the shared decision], the information may need to make [fact or distinction] verifiable under [conditions], because [source evidence or stated responsibility]. The remaining question is [uncertainty].”

Use stronger language when appropriate evidence already establishes the requirement. Keep the provenance visible. A public account can establish that a difficulty occurred. A technical source can establish a product condition. A review-policy document can establish a formal requirement. Their combination may support an inference; actual prevalence and applicability still require the evidence suitable to those claims. [[R01]](#vwx14kzg8vk)

### State what the research added

Identify what changed in your understanding: the concern you supplied, any new distinction or source the tool introduced, what the source check confirms, and the question still unresolved. When the response repeats an uncertainty already in the prompt, record that as supplied context.

Classify a repeated prompt uncertainty as supplied context. Then identify the extra work the response contributes: a useful decomposition, external condition, source, or challenge. In Asteron, a role prompt can likewise contain the concern that later dominates the search. Treat that concern as supplied context; count as an addition the new distinction, source, method, threshold, or unresolved condition the research introduces. [[R06]](#bpfxj86y64sj)

A completed record can leave the human requirement hypothetical and still provide a useful result: a verified source distinction, a corrected description of the available evidence, or a precise question for one of the people involved. That is a valid endpoint for an information-only investigation.

**WHAT TO DO** Finish each role page with the strongest source-grounded statement, one next check, and the questions left open. Keep its information requirement and proposed intervention effect separately classified.

## 7. COMPARE FINDINGS AND PLAN WHAT TO SHARE

### Put the responsibility findings together

Put the findings for the represented responsibilities alongside one another. Identify shared facts, different uses of those facts, and information someone will need from another person. Keep all of these findings connected to the same decision.

### What is shared?

Several roles may depend on the same product fact, contract term, study, or test. A shared source can provide a common factual basis. Inspect whether the roles use the same passage and whether its conditions remain intact in each answer.

### What does the baseline show?

The role-agnostic baseline retains the shared situation and evidence request. In Asteron, the Neutral conditions show what the same shared situation and decision moment produced without an explicit organizational role. Use matched Neutral comparisons to describe how the role-framed source set differed; separating the causal contribution of title, accountability wording, and other prompt features requires further tests. [[R06]](#bpfxj86y64sj)

### What differs?

One person may need to establish that something is possible. Another may need to judge whether it fits their constraints. A third may need to confirm cost, timing, eligibility, risk, or whether an appropriate review occurred. These are different uses of information within one shared decision. [R01]

### What information must be carried to the next person?

Name the recipient and what they need to receive: the supporting source, date, applicable conditions, limitations, open questions, and any relevant verification or approval. This transfer is a handoff. It can be a conversation or a document. Record the person's actual need where known and identify any proposed need as a hypothesis. [R01]

| **Cross-role finding** | **What it may suggest checking next** |
| --- | --- |
| Several role-framed answers rely on the same fact | Whether one current reference could preserve the common evidence and its conditions. |
| Role-framed answers cite different pages about one criterion | Whether the pages establish different things, describe different conditions, or repeat the same underlying evidence. |
| A proposed handoff needs a condition missing from an answer | Whether the source contains it and how the handoff should preserve it. |
| Recommendations converge while cited sets diverge | Whether the shared recommendation follows from the supplied situation; which different source claims each answer uses; and what people would need to examine. Record source overlap and answer agreement separately. |
| A proposed requirement has thin support | Which source, specialist, or practitioner check would most improve the account. |

Use these findings to select the next question to investigate. Existing information may already be adequate. The next useful step may be a conversation, a specialist check, a private record, or a decision to maintain the material already in use.

### Write a summary someone else can examine

A useful readout states the shared decision, the responsibilities examined, the main source findings, the provisional requirements, what remains open about the people, and the unknowns most likely to change the decision. It also records where the available evidence is adequate and what would justify additional work.

WHAT TO DO Summarize what the comparisons and source checks established. Identify what someone else needs to examine, what remains uncertain, and the next question to resolve before proposing a change.

## 8. CHOOSE WHAT TO CHECK OR CHANGE NEXT

### Choose the next action based on the question that remains

The investigation should now give you a more specific account of the shared decision: the responsibilities involved, the information found, what the inspected sources support, and what remains uncertain. Choose the next action according to the question it would help resolve and how the answer could affect the decision. Sometimes the available information is already adequate for the next conversation. [[R01-R03]](#vwx14kzg8vk)

Use the parts of this section that fit your next step. You may finish with a source check or a private discussion. Interviews, information production, and publication are possible continuations when they address a specific need; the original research remains useful on its own.

| **What needs attention?** | **A useful next step** |
| --- | --- |
| A claim's support or applicability | Inspect the original source and its conditions. Ask the factual owner or qualified specialist to resolve remaining questions. |
| A person's priorities or constraints | Ask that person. Use the research to prepare specific questions and record which assumptions they confirm, qualify, or change. |
| Experience of a comparable decision | Interview people with relevant experience. Ask what happened, what evidence they used, what they chose, and what followed. |
| Information that is hard to use | Correct, explain, organize, or document it so the intended people can find, understand, verify, or share it. |
| Whether a change helps | State the expected improvement and arrange an appropriate test. Assess usefulness to people separately from AI retrieval or citation. |

### Use QFOs and inspected pages to prepare the questions

Start with the priority leads recorded in Step 5. These include recurring retrieved-but-uncited pages, especially where a QFO explicitly requested material associated with their domain. Use the passages you inspected to prepare questions for people who can explain a practice, substantiate a claim, describe relevant experience, or challenge an account. [[R28, R29]](#v32n49r29wbp)

A QFO provides the exact mechanical request: perhaps a comparison, measurement method, condition, or documented experience. Reading the returned pages may reveal additional questions inside their text. Record these origins separately. A question raised by a forum discussion is a source-reading finding; it becomes an observed QFO only when the query log actually contains it.

For each lead, record what the search requested, what the inspected material already answers, and what remains unresolved for the shared decision. Keep the run ID, exact query, URL, and passage with your proposed question. Where query-to-URL linkage is unavailable, label the connection as your analysis of the run. [[R06, R07]](#bpfxj86y64sj)

Read answers and substantive disagreements as well as unanswered questions. A useful interview can establish the circumstances of a reported success, examine a disputed number, or compare a practice with another person's experience. Frame the question so the respondent can qualify or contradict the original account. A respondent's credentials do not verify the earlier account; their independently supported contribution needs its own record.

Include questions raised by participants, specialist knowledge, or your examination of the decision even when they are absent from the QFOs. Ask whether an answer could change how people assess their options. Record whether the question came from an observed search, a source passage, a participant, or an investigator's proposed concern. [[R29]](#p9gwd6ae34mi)

### Ask people who can supply the missing information

Some questions concern the people making this particular decision. Their preferences, available time, commitments, and willingness to accept a trade-off need to come from them. Use the research to make that conversation specific and preserve each person's ability to correct your account of their part in the choice. [[R01]](#vwx14kzg8vk)

Other questions concern how people have handled a comparable decision. Ask a subject-matter expert or experienced respondent about a particular event: what they needed to establish, which evidence they consulted, what uncertainty remained, what they chose, and what followed. Their experience may inform the present decision while its applicability remains a separate judgment. [[R28, R29]](#v32n49r29wbp)

Follow up on dates, circumstances, direct involvement, and supporting records. Distinguish recollection, interpretation, and statements checked against documents. A person can describe a decision they took; a stronger claim about what caused its outcome needs further evidence.

For several interviews, use a shared set of questions about comparable circumstances, followed by questions specific to each respondent's responsibility. Ask everyone what they needed to establish, what evidence they used, what they decided, what would make them reconsider, and who else contributed. Save the question set and record changes between rounds. This makes similarities and differences easier to examine without assuming every respondent faced identical conditions. [[R29]](#p9gwd6ae34mi)

When contact is unavailable, preserve the source findings and specify the human question that remains open. The research can still be complete for the information-side question it was designed to answer. [[R01, R02]](#vwx14kzg8vk)

### Asteron example: ask about actual funding decisions

**Proposed continuation.** The existing Asteron research records different questions and sources under the CMO, CFO, Legal, and Content prompts. Scope. One run per condition. The full 81-condition matrix supplies the broader descriptive context. Repeatability, cross-system behavior, and human usefulness remain separate evidence questions.

### In some prior tests, identifiable authoritative sources concentrated the cited source set

Observed / documented. In the cross-setting tests, the risk-review role drew heavily on formal sources in the cases classified as having an authoritative source for the concern. The source analysis used a ratio of canon pages to canon-plus-vendor pages. It reported values from 0.75 to 1.0 in those cases and 0.0 in the two software cases classified as lacking a comparable source. Other source classes sit outside that ratio, so it is a compositional measure rather than the share of all citations. [[R10]](#5rtw7zw4syti)

The software results were especially informative. The model cited vendor terms, export guidance, and related documentation. The available general review material did not become an equivalent cited substitute for those particular questions. [[R10]](#5rtw7zw4syti)

Interpretation. A useful source relationship is often specific to the thing being established. Vendor documentation can be primary evidence for published terms. An official source can establish a requirement. A test can establish an observed result under stated conditions. Those functions can complement each other.

Scope and next evidence. The classification of an authority being present or absent was part of the research design and analysis. It is not a legal finding that a risk is wholly unregulated. The studies also varied substantive topics and had limited repetition within each setting. The proposed explanation is a bounded pattern to test more broadly.

### Retrieval is much broader than visible citation

Observed / documented. In Asteron, Reddit supplied 2,111 run-by-URL retrieval records and Wikipedia supplied 614. Neither domain had a cited record in that batch. The same findings report 214 retrieved records and 76 cited records for Pew Research Center, and 711 retrieved records and 82 cited records for arXiv. These are counts of source appearances across runs, not counts of distinct people or measures of influence. [[R06, R07]](#bpfxj86y64sj)

Earlier studies also recorded substantial community retrieval with little citation. However, the recovered record includes Reddit and Wikipedia citations in some prior conditions. Academic material likewise changed markedly by topic: a weakly cited source class in some product-selection studies became a recurring cited source in the Asteron AI-search discussion. [[R09, R14]](#89td87dl9dir)

Interpretation. Citation selection depends on more than encounter frequency. The relevance of the particular page, the question being answered, and the evidentiary job are worth inspecting.

Scope and next evidence. These observations identify neither a permanent source blacklist nor the internal reason for selecting one source over another. Credibility, applicability, redundancy, available snippets, and answer construction remain possible explanations. Establishing influence would require additional evidence beyond retrieval or co-occurrence.

### In Asteron, broad recommendations converged while cited source sets differed

Observed / documented. The Asteron analyst’s answer screen reports that the runs directly asked to choose among funding actions converged on forms of capped, redesigned, better-instrumented continuation. The same report records no recommendation for immediate expansion or outright termination across its reviewed answers. Meanwhile, the cited source sets differed substantially by condition. [[R06]](#bpfxj86y64sj)

This is the analyst-runner’s reported answer screen. The archived corpus and mixed-review description limit detailed answer-category claims; Appendix J states those limits. [[R06, R08]](#bpfxj86y64sj)

Interpretation. Agreement at the level of a broad recommendation can coexist with different evidence paths. A bibliography comparison and an answer comparison answer different questions.

Scope and next evidence. The synthetic scenario supplied improving visibility, small direct referrals, and uncertain downstream effects. Those conditions may have favored bounded continuation. The study therefore gives an example of convergence; it leaves open how frequently this happens in other situations.

For the role-by-role investigation, a shared broad recommendation still leaves distinct work to support. ### A sequence of questions built the research program

The early studies noticed different source selections around different role-framed questions. The research changed its working explanations. Job-title language gave way to closer examination of responsibility and specificity. An expected fallback to independent review media was contradicted in the software tests, where vendor documentation was selected instead. [[R09, R10]](#89td87dl9dir)

### The Asteron design in plain language

The experiment used one frozen synthetic business record. Its $420,000 pilot cost, $120,000 typical first-year contract value, 48% gross margin, visibility gains, and uncertain downstream results were invented inputs. The observed result was how AI researched and answered those inputs. [[R05]](#pjeuoaw8c5z5)

Each prompt level retained the prior material and added context. A common evidence request supported comparison. After a six-condition Neutral/CFO pilot, the batch completed all 81 conditions on September 8, 2026, using the model recorded as gpt-5.6 with OpenAI API web search through Thinking Inspector, with documented coverage and preservation limits. There was one run per exact condition. Jobs ran concurrently, with ordering preserved within jobs. [[R04, R06, R08]](#ifimbnt8y3w0)

## APPENDIX D — PROMPT CONSTRUCTION

### Preserve ancestry and make the changes inspectable

The protocol describes five conceptual components: situation facts; role and accountability; decision moment; the knowledge addition; and a constant evidence request. The Neutral conditions retain the organizational situation while leaving the explicit role identity out. [[R04]](#ifimbnt8y3w0)

L2 was designed as an additive extension of L1, and L3 as an additive extension of L2. The purpose was to let the analysis ask which new behavior appeared when additional facts entered the question. The highest level supplied decision conditions rather than a prescribed bibliography or a list of specialist methods to invoke.

The constant close asked for current external evidence where relevant, citations to relied-on sources, a distinction between external evidence and inference about the synthetic company, and visible uncertainty where stronger conclusions lacked support. Thus, the experiment explicitly requested evidence; results describe that prompting condition.

The role conditions included responsibility-specific work. Consequently, a comparison with Neutral measures the constructed role/accountability prompt, including its relevant wording and concerns. A bare-title effect would require a separate manipulation.

Longer knowledge prompts also change length and the number of supplied premises. Additive construction reduces arbitrary rewriting, while leaving those features coupled to the knowledge manipulation. The reported comparisons should be read with that fact in view.

For reproducibility, the exact executed workbook version, model, evidence request, and run settings matter. For human-facing examples, condensed paraphrases should be labeled as paraphrases and kept distinct from the executed instrument. The Product X stress test used the Revision 3 template, with its executed wording preserved in the frozen prompt file. Use that file for replication; this edition's adaptable template has been edited for broader use. [R20]

## APPENDIX F — ANALYTICAL METHODS

### Source-event normalization

The source-event artifact has one row for each run and original URL combination. It preserves role, knowledge level, decision moment, run identifier, original URL, mechanical hostname and domain fields, and source-state flags. The Asteron schema reports 81 run IDs, 9,690 rows, and 3,443 distinct original URLs. The Product X stress-test table reports 4 run IDs, 288 rows, and 241 distinct original URLs. Domain derivation is a mechanical heuristic; editorial source families are a separate analytical choice. [[R07, R20]](#3wzhf4b96orf)

Original URL strings and normalized groupings answer different questions. Exact-address overlap retains distinctions between pages and variants. Hostname or publisher-family views group sources more broadly. A reproducible comparison states which grouping it uses and how aliases, subdomains, and URL variants are handled, and does so before counting.

### Descriptive measures and denominators

A run’s source count measures the size of its recorded source set. A domain’s recurrence can mean runs containing the domain or the number of run-by-URL rows from it. Those counts should be labeled separately. Calculator actions are separated from web-search queries when the trace supports that distinction.

Asteron’s reported distinct-source fraction is 213 / 3,443, approximately 6.2%. Its run-by-URL citation fraction is 742 / 9,690, approximately 7.7%. The former counts each address once across the batch. The latter counts an address again when it appears in another run. Neither fraction measures accuracy, influence, or human usefulness. [[R06, R07]](#bpfxj86y64sj)

Historical studies used pooled, per-run, domain-specific, and source-kind-specific denominators. Their percentages remain attached to their original units. This note treats their shared directional pattern as useful while leaving a harmonized pooled estimate for a separate analysis. [[R09]](#89td87dl9dir)

### Matched comparisons

The Asteron analysis compares each role with Neutral at the same knowledge level and decision moment. It also compares levels within a role and decision moment, and decision moments within a role and level. Aggregating the resulting comparisons by role describes patterns within this matrix. It supplies neither independent repeat runs for an exact cell nor a population estimate across all possible prompts.

### Sources used together and sources shared across answers

The comparisons in this note use two bibliometric ideas that answer different questions. Co-citation (Small, 1973) counts sources cited together within one answer and reads their co-occurrence as relatedness for that purpose. Bibliographic coupling (Kessler, 1963) counts the sources two answers share and reads that as similarity between the answers. The Jaccard figures in Appendix G and in the Product X specimen are coupling measures between answers. Within-answer co-citation remains a candidate analysis of the historical source-event rows. [[R23]](#c95czyn3bejz)

This application compares deliberately generated answers. In a matched role contrast, the recorded system, shared situation, and evidence request are fixed while responsibility wording varies. The comparison describes those outputs; wider claims require further evidence.

### Source-set overlap

Jaccard similarity divides shared items by the distinct items in the union. Report both set sizes. When useful, also report the overlap coefficient: shared items divided by the smaller set. In the Product X stress-test specimen, reviewer and Procurement have reported values of 0.176 and 0.375, from three shared URLs. In the separate Asteron study, Legal has 121 summed cited run-by-URL appearances, compared with 69-92 for the other roles. Those totals alone leave the contribution of set size to the matched mean unresolved. Preserve the counting unit and normalization rule. [[R06, R20]](#bpfxj86y64sj)

Low overlap can coexist with agreement on a claim. High overlap can coexist with different interpretations. Answer review therefore remains separate. Both-empty citation sets should be recorded as an absence of cited evidence rather than silently treated as evidence agreement; the present selected tables concern nonempty reported comparisons.

### Query analysis and source classification

The analyses examined query vocabulary, domain-scoped searches, and recurring source families. Token overlap describes wording. A semantic interpretation of what the query seeks is a further analytical step. The older co-query work explicitly distinguishes vocabulary, query purpose, and citation structure, and reports limitations in its heuristic labels and pooled role mapping. [[R16]](#7t4rmnjxhwkm)

Source-kind categories such as vendor, official, community, and review media were study-specific classifications. The page’s function can differ from the publisher’s general identity. Source-kind comparisons therefore require their original coding rules and local scope. Instrument-supplied site-type labels are retained as-is and not used for source-kind claims.

### Answer screen and synthesis

The Asteron report describes a sentence-level screen plus manual review of early outputs. Its aggregate conclusion is that funding-choice answers favored bounded continuation. The available archive and mixed-review details limit how strongly fine-grained category counts can be used. This note carries the summary as the analyst’s reported finding and gives the unresolved items in Appendix J. [[R06, R08]](#bpfxj86y64sj)

The final synthesis relates source behavior to proposed publishing choices. It supplies hypotheses for intervention selection. Establishing an intervention’s effect requires a baseline, an appropriate comparison, and a record of other relevant changes, using the [<u>AI Visibility</u>](https://citationlabs.com/operating-system/field-guides/ai-visibility/) or Information Design method as appropriate. [[R01, R02]](#vwx14kzg8vk)

## APPENDIX H — HOW OUR INTERPRETATIONS CHANGED

### Five interpretations that changed as more evidence was added

From role label to specified work. Early observations associated different role prompts with different source sets. Specificity and accountability probes then showed that the content of the question matters alongside the label. The public practice therefore begins with the required proposition and responsibility, while treating title-only personalization as a separate hypothesis. [[R09]](#89td87dl9dir)

From sector label to the particular evidence requirement. Early industrial-versus-clinical comparisons suggested different source mixtures. Later committee and question-level tests showed important differences within those settings. The public practice assigns source roles question by question. [[R09, R11]](#89td87dl9dir)

From expected review-media substitution to an observed vendor-documentation route. The software tests contradicted the expectation that the risk-review role would automatically use general independent media when a canonical source was absent. It selected vendors’ own documents. The effect of purpose-built independent examination remains open. [[R10]](#5rtw7zw4syti)

From a universal citation percentage to defined local rates. Historical summaries used a narrow approximate band, but the underlying studies mixed denominators and source kinds. Asteron reports distinct-URL and run-by-URL fractions separately. The public practice preserves those units and treats source selection as local to the observed question and environment. [[R07, R09, R14]](#3wzhf4b96orf)

From role-only interpretation to inspection of the shared record. The Product X stress-test specimen combined an unspecified governing regime and a synthetic product; all 73 recorded search queries were site-scoped to formal-source hosts, and no vendor, trade, community, or review-media page surfaced. Those shared inputs are plausible contributors to that pattern; their separate effects remain untested. In a live project, name the governing context when it is known and remember that a real product identity makes product-specific documentation available to the research system. [R20]

### Two additional cautions retained in this edition

The source record documents model-version changes and a pilot interpretation that failed to hold in the full Asteron matrix. Both argue for keeping versions, dates, and the exact compared conditions visible. [[R06, R09]](#bpfxj86y64sj)

Earlier analytical prose sometimes treats retrieval as influence or citation absence as rejection. This note keeps those as possible interpretations to investigate. Its reported observations remain retrieval, explicit tool actions where available, citation, and answer content.
